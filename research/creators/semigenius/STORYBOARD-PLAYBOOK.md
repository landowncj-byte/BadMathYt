# STORYBOARD PLAYBOOK — what the Semigenius study teaches us

Written 2026-11-25 from `videos.md` in this folder. Owner's brief: "The narration doesn't have to be the best if the storyboarding is just amazing… the animation simple enough that most people can understand exactly what's going on. The whole goal is to make this accessible to a lot of people."

**Evidence base (be honest about it):** 44 Shorts exist. 5 were watched frame by frame (the 5 newest; one of them has 1.10M views), 5 more (the 5 most-viewed, 1.25M to 3.27M) were read from 1 fps thumbnails and captions. The other 34 are known by title, views and captions only. Everything below was visible in all 10. Shot-length numbers come from 5 videos.

## 1. Why their storyboards tell the whole story

1. **The script is a list of pictures.** Every spoken clause is a physical action in the present tense ("you throw a sheet over the dog"). So each clause has exactly one obvious picture, and the picture is on screen while the clause is spoken. The narration never explains, never summarises, never talks about something that is not on screen.
2. **Frame 0 is the whole problem in one picture.** The hero, the thing in his way and the rule or number are all in the first frame (a man, a dog, a `NO PETS ALLOWED` sign). You know the story before the first word ends.
3. **One idea per shot, about one shot per second.** Average shot 1.1 to 1.4 s, nearly none over 2.5 s. A shot is held longer only when the picture itself is the joke.
4. **Wide first, then close.** A new place always starts with one wide frame containing one landmark or one sign. Only then do they cut to hands and faces.
5. **Cause, then effect, in neighbouring shots.** Bump → stumble → bus → flight. A cause is planted in the frame before it acts (the bus enters the same frame as the victim; the thermometer hangs on the wall two shots before it matters).
6. **The hero is always findable.** Same costume in every shot of every video, same side of the frame within a video, the only thing of that colour in the picture.
7. **Almost nothing is on screen.** 2 or 3 things to read: the hero, one other character or object, one prop or sign. Rooms contain only what the story touches.
8. **Numbers, time and scale are things you can see.** A dial with the needle on 20. A height line on the wall. A counter stuck to the spinning object. Grey hair and a walking frame for "sixty years". A pull-out to the whole town for "everybody".
9. **Feelings are drawn large and held.** A face change in close-up, plus one comic mark (heart, tears, stars, starburst), for about a second after every event.
10. **The last picture answers the first.** The sign is reversed, the roles are swapped, the pose repeats.

**How much does the picture carry?** In the 5 watched reels I could retell the plot from the frames with the captions covered. Roughly 85 to 90 % picture; the voice adds the jokes' wording and a few names.

## 2. Rules we can apply

Each rule is written so it can be checked on a storyboard row.

**A. The mute test (the master rule)**
- A1. For every shot, write one plain sentence: "what does a viewer with no sound and no captions understand from this picture?" If the sentence is empty, vague ("he reacts"), or needs a word from the narration to make sense, redraw the shot.
- A2. Read the mute-test column top to bottom. It must tell the full story to a ten-year-old: who, what they want, what they do, what goes wrong, how it ends.
- A3. A tag or sign may NAME a thing (a place, a year, a role). It may never be the only carrier of an EVENT. "The sale is cancelled" needs a stamp landing and the bottle being carried away, not a tag that says CANCELLED.

**B. One idea per shot**
- B1. One shot = one action or one fact. If the row needs "and" between two events, split it.
- B2. One number per shot, alone in the frame with the thing it measures.
- B3. A shot is a picture of the clause being spoken right now. Not the clause before, not the next one.

**C. Establish, then detail**
- C1. Every new place opens with a wide (≤ 2.5 s) that shows one landmark or one sign of 1 to 3 words. No close-up in a place we have not seen.
- C2. Every new character first appears whole (head to toe, or at least waist up) with their tell visible, before any close-up.
- C3. A thing that will matter later is in the frame at least one shot before it acts (the planted prop).
- C4. When a problem depends on a layout (upstairs/downstairs, this room/that room, here/there), show the whole layout in ONE frame: a cut-away, a top view or a map.

**D. Cause → effect**
- D1. Cause and effect are two neighbouring shots, cause first, or one shot where both are in frame.
- D2. The effect shot starts within 10 frames of the cause.
- D3. Every effect is followed by a face: who is glad, who is scared.
- D4. Never show an effect whose cause was only spoken.

**E. Keep it findable**
- E1. Each main character owns one colour, one silhouette tell and one side of the frame for the whole video. Flip sides only when the story flips (the winner becomes the loser).
- E2. Direction means something and stays the same: toward the goal is one way, away is the other. Things that come back, come back the opposite way.
- E3. After a cut, the subject is within the same third of the frame, or the cut is a deliberate jump to a new place (then C1 applies).

**F. Keep it empty**
- F1. At most **3 things to read** in a frame: subject, object, one context cue (a sign, a prop, a number). Captions do not count; everything else does.
- F2. At most one sign or tag per shot, 1 to 3 words. A second tag needs a second shot.
- F3. Background figures are one mass (a row of heads), never individuals with their own business.
- F4. If a prop is not touched, pointed at or used as a landmark, delete it.

**G. Show numbers, time and scale**
- G1. A number lives on an instrument or object in the scene (dial, board, tag, tape, stack) and is the biggest thing in its shot.
- G2. A big number also gets a size picture: a pile next to a person, rows to the vanishing point, a pull-out. The viewer should feel "that is a lot" before reading the digits.
- G3. Time passing = the same framing, changed (same shop, grey hair; calendar pages; day/night through a window). Never a caption alone.
- G4. "How many" = count them on screen (37 empty slots, 3 photos) when the number is small; rows or a mass when it is large.
- G5. A change in a number is shown changing (the tile flips, the pile shrinks), not as before/after text.

**H. Recurring symbols**
- H1. Give every recurring idea one symbol and never vary it: the liar's mark, the money, "this is fake", "this is the law", "this is the year". Introduce each symbol once, clearly, then reuse it silently.
- H2. No more than 6 symbols per video. Each must be recognisable as a silhouette.
- H3. The ending reuses the opening's symbol, changed (the sign reversed, the pile gone).

**I. Feelings**
- I1. One clear face per beat, in close-up, held 0.8 to 1.4 s. Add one comic mark when the feeling must read at thumbnail size (sweat, heart, stars, tears).
- I2. Thoughts are a single picture in a thought cloud, or an object held beside the face. Never words.

**J. The hook picture**
- J1. Frame 0 contains the hero (or villain), the object of desire and the conflict, readable with no words in under half a second.
- J2. Shot 2 arrives within 2 s and turns the situation (the other side acts).
- J3. At least 3 shots in the first 4 s.

**K. Cuts**
- K1. Hard cuts by default. A fancier transition must carry meaning (a match cut that says "this is the same bottle").
- K2. Average shot ≤ 2.4 s for our calmer narration (theirs is 1.1 to 1.4 s). A shot over 3 s must have a second action inside it.

## 3. Review checklist for a storyboard

Tick every line, or fix the board.

- [ ] **M1 Mute read-through.** The mute-test column, read alone, tells the full story to a child. No sentence is empty or leans on the narration.
- [ ] **M2 Frame 0.** Hero/villain + object + conflict in one picture.
- [ ] **M3 One idea.** No row holds two events or two numbers.
- [ ] **M4 Wide first.** Every new place opens on a wide with one landmark or sign; every new character is first seen whole.
- [ ] **M5 Planted props.** Everything that acts was visible at least one shot earlier.
- [ ] **M6 Cause → effect → face.** Every event has its cause in the shot before (or the same shot) and a reaction right after.
- [ ] **M7 Three things.** No frame asks the eye to read more than 3 things; at most one tag of ≤ 3 words.
- [ ] **M8 Findable.** Each main character keeps colour, tell and side; direction of travel is consistent.
- [ ] **M9 Numbers on things.** Every number is on an object, alone in its shot, with a size picture for the big ones; time passing is a changed repeat of a framing.
- [ ] **M10 Symbols.** ≤ 6 recurring symbols, each introduced once and never varied.
- [ ] **M11 Feelings.** Each beat has one readable face, held.
- [ ] **M12 Bookend.** The last picture reuses and changes the first.
- [ ] **M13 Delete test.** For each shot: if it were removed, would the mute story lose a step? If not, cut or merge it.
- [ ] **M14 Animatic on mute.** Before the full render, play the silent animatic to someone who has not read the script and ask them to tell the story back.

## 4. What NOT to copy

- **Their character:** the blue-shirt, two-dot-eyes hero, the bald landlord, any of their cast.
- **Their art:** the low-poly 3D look, their sets, their caption styling.
- **Their words:** "being a semigenius", "I fixed it. It's worse now.", "send this to…" lines as written, the swearing.
- **Their stories and gags:** every plot in `videos.md`, including the film homages.
- **Their humour:** crude, bodily, often cruel. Bad Math is true stories with real victims; our victims are never the joke.
- **Their relationship to facts:** the real answer is hidden in the description and the video is fantasy. Ours is the reverse: everything on screen is a sourced fact or clearly tagged as our illustration or our maths.
- **Their escalation-to-absurdity spine** as a story structure for Decoded. We keep our own structure (hook → players → money → tell → trick → bill → verdict). What we take is how each step is DRAWN.

## 5. What transfers to Bad Math, and what needs care

| Their habit | Ours |
|---|---|
| Narration describes the picture | Our narration is final before the board. So the board must find the one picture each clause already implies, and when a clause is abstract ("his family didn't start making that wine until 1982") the board must invent a physical picture for it (37 empty year-slots, then the first bottle). |
| Shots of about 1.2 s | Owen is calmer; 1.5 to 2.4 s stays our range. Do not chase their speed. |
| Signs carry places and rules | Our mono tags and name plates do the same job, but we have used too many. Rule F2 cuts them to one per shot. |
| No numbers on screen at all in most videos | We are a numbers channel. Their best number video (height line, counters on the object) is the model: number on the thing, changing in front of you. |
| Comic marks for feelings | We already have them as "outline cues" in the face reservoir. Use them more in wides, where a face is small. |
| Cut-away doll's house | Fits our pop-up paper sets naturally: a set can fold open. |
