# Allen Stanford — "He landed a helicopter at Lord's with $20 million in a box. It was your money."

**Access note:** WebSearch extracts only (DOJ pages appeared in results; extracts read). Grades ✔2/✔1/⚠.

## Story beats
1. **Antigua (Caribbean) / Houston.** Texan **R. Allen Stanford** owns **Stanford International Bank**, an offshore bank in Antigua that sells **certificates of deposit (CDs)** — a CD is a savings deal: you lock money up and the bank promises a fixed return (✔2).
2. The bank promises **returns above what normal banks pay**, said to be backed by "safe" investments (prosecutors: "inflated returns" ✔2).
3. **Show-off era:** Stanford sponsors cricket; in **2008** he launches the **Stanford Super Series**; he reportedly lands at **Lord's** (London's famous cricket ground) **by helicopter** and unveils a **box he says holds $20 million** for a winner-takes-all match (✔1 → "reportedly"; verify with a second source before voicing).
4. **Tell:** the CDs aren't earning the promised money — **new customers' deposits pay old customers** who cash out, and a chunk of money goes to Stanford (prosecutors: "Ponzi" per DOJ/jury ✔2; ~$2bn siphoned per one outlet ⚠).
5. **Feb 2009:** the SEC sues, alleging a "massive ongoing fraud" (✔2). The cricket series collapses.
6. **6 March 2012:** federal jury in Houston convicts him on **13 of 14 counts** after a ~6-week trial (✔2: DOJ/ABA Journal/Al Jazeera).
7. **14 June 2012:** sentenced to **110 years** (Judge David Hittner); prosecutors had asked for 230, defense about 41 months; **money judgment $5.9 billion** (✔2: DOJ, DOL, Al Jazeera).
8. Payoff: the man who flashed $20 million in a box was running a scheme that, per the jury, took **more than $7 billion** from CD holders over 20 years.

## Story loop
STAKES: a bank with the best savings rates anywhere. · QUESTION: how is a small island bank paying more than giant banks? · TWIST: it wasn't — the box of cash was paid for by the next depositor. · REHOOKS: "a helicopter at Lord's" → "twenty million in a box" → "who pays the old customers?" → "110 years".
