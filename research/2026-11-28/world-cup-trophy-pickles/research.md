# The World Cup trophy stolen in 1966 — "Someone stole the World Cup. A dog found it."
Decoded · Heist (sport; Pickles the dog) · compiled 2026-11-26 for Fri 2026-12-04 11:00

**Access note:** WebSearch extracts only. Grades ✔2/✔1/⚠. Fun/family-safe; thief identity is unsettled — this is a "what happened and what the courts did" story.

## Story beats
1. **March 1966, London,** four months before England hosts the World Cup. The golden **Jules Rimet trophy** is on display at a stamp exhibition (**Stampex, Westminster Central Hall**) (✔2: Wikipedia extract, SI/Irish Times).
2. **Sun 20 March 1966:** it is **stolen from the display** (✔2; "afternoon" ✔1).
3. A **ransom demand of £15,000** arrives with **part of the trophy's lining** to prove it, from a man calling himself **"Jackson"** (✔1-2: Wikipedia extract, Irish Times; date of note ⚠ 21 vs 23 March).
4. Police arrest **Edward Betchley**, a London dockworker (✔2). He claims to be only a middleman and **never reveals the thief**. He is convicted of **demanding money with menaces** and gets **two years** (concurrent sentences) (✔1-2 → "two years" safe; charge wording varies by outlet: handling stolen goods / extortion ⚠ → say "convicted over the ransom demand"). He died in **1969** (✔1).
5. **27 March 1966** (one week after): **David Corbett** walks his dog **Pickles** in south London and the dog sniffs out a package under a hedge — **the trophy wrapped in newspaper** (✔2: SI, Irish Times, Wikipedia extract). Corbett is briefly a suspect (✔2).
6. Corbett collects a **reward — nearly £5,000** (✔1 Wikipedia; others differ → say "a reward of several thousand pounds").
7. **July 1966:** England wins at Wembley with the trophy back (well-known; not sourced here — skip or "that summer England won it").
8. Twist/payoff: the original thief was never convicted — the case ended with a middleman's two years and a dog. (A later claim names a London gangster as the thief ⚠ — do NOT say.)
