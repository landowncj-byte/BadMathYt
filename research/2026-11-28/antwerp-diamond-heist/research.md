## Story beats
1. **Antwerp, Belgium** is the world's diamond capital. A vault under the Antwerp Diamond Centre sits behind layered security. An Italian man, **Leonardo Notarbartolo**, rents an office in the building posing as a diamond dealer — so he can walk in and look around for about two years (rent + "dealer" cover ✔2; "two years" ✔1 → say "for a long time").
2. **Weekend of 15–16 Feb 2003:** a small Italian crew (press nicknames "the School of Turin") gets into the vault and empties safe-deposit boxes. Estimated haul **about $100 million** (✔2; most never recovered ✔2).
3. They plan to destroy all evidence — burn the rubbish. Instead the bag is dumped by a roadside near Brussels (E19 motorway area ✔1-2). **The twist:** that bag holds the proof (including a half-eaten salami sandwich with Notarbartolo's DNA, per Wikipedia extract ✔1-2).
4. Police also use CCTV from a shop where the sandwich was bought (✔1 — "reported"; say "police say").
5. **21 Feb 2003 (five days after):** Notarbartolo is arrested when he returns to the Diamond Centre (✔2).
6. **2005:** Antwerp court — Notarbartolo **10 years** as organiser; three accomplices (Tavano, Finotto, D'Onorio) **5 years each** (✔2: Expatica 20 May 2005 "four jailed"; Wikipedia extract; Guinness-style summaries). Released on parole 2009 (✔1).
7. Payoff: the loot is still missing, but the man who planned it for years was caught by his own garbage.
