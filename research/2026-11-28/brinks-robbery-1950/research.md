# Great Brink's Robbery 1950 — "They kept the secret for six years. One guy broke it with days to spare."
Decoded · Heist (Boston's "crime of the century") · compiled 2026-11-26 for Thu 2026-12-03

**Access note:** WebSearch extracts only (FBI, Time, History.com, Wikipedia not openable). Grades ✔2/✔1/⚠.

## Story beats
1. **17 Jan 1950, Boston:** masked men (rubber Halloween masks, chauffeur caps, rubber-soled shoes ✔2) walk into the Brink's building, tie up the employees and walk out with the cash room's haul. Nobody is hurt. The FBI's evidence shelf later holds one of the **chauffeur caps** (✔2 FBI artifact + Time/Wikipedia "left rope and a cap").
2. **Haul:** about **$2.7 million** in cash, checks, money orders and securities (FBI "more than $2.7 million"; Time $2,775,395 ✔2); cash alone about **$1.2 million** (a 1956 headline: $1,218,211 ✔1).
3. For years police chase **"hundreds of dead ends"** (FBI section title ✔1) — they interrogate almost every known Boston criminal.
4. The gang makes a deal: **lie low for six years**, because the statute of limitations on the robbery runs out after six (✔2: Time/History/Smithsonian-type extracts).
5. **The crack:** one member, **Joseph "Specs" O'Keefe**, gets jailed for an unrelated burglary (Pennsylvania, 1950 ✔2). He feels cheated about his share and the others won't pay up (one account: he says he was shorted $62,000 ⚠ → don't voice). He confesses.
6. **Jan 1956:** with days left on the clock — **reported as 4–5 days** before the statute expired (✔2 but numbers differ: "four days" vs "five days" → say "less than a week") — an indictment of **11 men** comes down; arrests follow (✔2).
7. **Trial opens 6 Aug 1956; verdict 5 Oct 1956 (✔1-2).** Eight men get **life in prison** (✔2: Boston Magazine/Wikipedia/allthatsinteresting; two died before trial, one more before — "11 men" vs "8 convicted"). O'Keefe pleaded guilty, testified, got **4 years** and was out in 1960 (✔2).
8. Massachusetts later **extended the robbery statute from 6 to 9 years** because of this case (Time ✔1 → "reportedly").
9. **Payoff / twist:** only about **$58,000** of the loot was ever recovered (✔2: Yahoo/Time-type extracts: $58–60K).

## Story loop
STAKES: $2.7 million in 1950 walks out the door in under half an hour. · QUESTION: can anyone catch masked thieves who left almost nothing? · TWIST: they beat the cops for six years — and lost to a man sitting in jail who felt shortchanged. · REHOOKS: "masks and caps" → "six-year plan" → "Specs sits in jail" → "days before the deadline" → "life".

## Convictions
Eight men convicted 1956 (incl. Tony Pino, "Big Joe" McGinnis), life sentences; O'Keefe pleaded guilty 4 yrs. Anything about who "planned" it (Pino) is court-based/Time ✔2 — but use "prosecutors said".

## Don't say
