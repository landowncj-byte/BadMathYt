# Great Train Robbery 1963 — "They hid out playing Monopoly. With real money. Then the board turned them in."
Decoded · Heist · compiled 2026-11-26 for Wed 2026-12-02

**Access note:** same as above — WebSearch extracts only (Wikipedia, Britannica, BTP, Mental Floss unopened). Grades ✔2/✔1/⚠.

## Story beats
1. **8 Aug 1963, Britain:** a Royal Mail overnight train carries bags of used banknotes. A gang of about 15 stops it by tampering with the signals at **Bridego Bridge** (✔2).
2. They take **120 mail sacks — about £2.6 million** (✔2; Britannica says £2,600,000 in mostly used notes; bag count 120 of 128 ✔2).
3. They retreat to **Leatherslade Farm** to lie low and **play Monopoly using real stolen cash** (✔2; Mental Floss, others).
4. **The plan:** wipe everything, then have a man burn the farmhouse. He didn't — reportedly took the money instead (✔1 Gale/BTP-type account → "reportedly").
5. A local tip leads police to the farm (a herdsman's tip, ✔1 → "a tip"). Police find **fingerprints on a Monopoly board and a ketchup bottle** (✔2: BTP/Mental Floss/Wikipedia extract). Roger Cordrey arrested within days (timing ⚠ 1 day / 6 days / a week).
6. **Trial at Aylesbury, April 1964:** the main men get **up to 30 years**; total about 300+ years across the gang (✔2: Wikipedia/Britannica extract "most 30 years"; Shropshire Star ~307 yrs; one timeline says 24–30 ⚠ → say "up to thirty years").
7. One man (John Daly), whose prints were on the Monopoly set, **was acquitted** (✔1 Wikipedia/Mental Floss).
8. Ronnie Biggs escapes Wandsworth Prison **July 1965** (✔2: History.com, Britannica) and flees abroad. (Optional kicker; also in the Biggs photo.)
9. Payoff: the plan that took months fell to a board game and a ketchup bottle.

## Convictions
Roger Cordrey, Ronnie Biggs, Bruce Reynolds (arrested 1968) and others convicted at Aylesbury 1964 (Reynolds later, 1968 ✔1 → don't say sentence). Daly acquitted. Say "a dozen men" only if re-sourced → say "most of the gang".
