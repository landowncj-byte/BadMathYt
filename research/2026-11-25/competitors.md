# Competitor study — Bad Math niche (compiled 2026-11-24/25)

## Read this first: how reliable is this file?
- **This sandbox could not open YouTube, TikTok, Instagram or Social Blade.** Every number below comes from **search-result extracts of third-party trackers** (OutlierKit, vidIQ, NexLev, Playboard, HypeAuditor, Trendreport) and press articles. Trackers disagree with each other and lag by weeks.
- Because of that: **watch URLs are mostly missing** (I give the tracker page where the number was read and the channel URL; find the video by exact title on the channel's Videos/Shorts tab). **Hooks (first spoken line) could not be captured** — where "hook" is shown it is the *title*, which on these channels is also the thumbnail promise. Lengths are unknown unless stated.
- Confidence key: **A** = two trackers/press agree; **B** = one tracker; **C** = single weak source or internally inconsistent. Nothing is invented; unknowns say "unknown".
- TikTok/Instagram-native data is the thinnest part. Treat sections on those platforms as directional. **To do properly, someone with a browser should spend 30 min on each channel's "Popular" tab** and fill the hook column.

## 3. Patterns — what is working now

**Topics and villain types**
1. **An industry the viewer personally uses, framed as rigged against him**: sportsbooks/gambling (Coffeezilla 9.3–9.9M), airlines/brands' fine print (Kullberg, Hydroflask Short), banks. Beats abstract "a guy stole money" stories.
2. **The villain everyone already knows** + "follow the money": Epstein money (two separate channels' top recent videos), Trump crypto, Elizabeth Holmes as a *reference point* ("The New Elizabeth Holmes").
3. **Rise-and-fall of a famous rich person** (Branson 17×, Rockefeller 10×).
4. **The viewer as the mark**: "Your Job Achieves Nothing", "You're probably losing $X a year doing this" (hook formula named in OutlierKit's finance teardown), Caleb Hammer roasting ordinary spending.
5. **Scale visualised physically** (rice, silver bar, iPhone).

**Hook formulas (real titles quoted)**
- Self-own: "Youtuber *Accidentally Exposes* the Scam He's Promoting"
- Borrowed fame: "The *New* Elizabeth Holmes"
- Open mystery: "We (Still) Don't Know How Epstein Got So Rich..."
- Insult + hedge: "Your Job Achieves Nothing... (probably)"
- Superlative: "World's *First* Billionaire", "Financial Audit's *Most* Abusive Husband"
- Secret: "What Hydroflask *Doesn't Want You To Know*"
- Dated plan: "If I Started Investing In *2026*…"
- Exhaustive list: "*Every* Government Form Explained in 12 Minutes"
- Epidemic: "Exposing the Gambling *Epidemic*"

**Titles / thumbnails:** 4–9 words; one proper noun or household brand; one superlative or one number; parenthetical hedge is common. (Thumbnails could not be viewed here.)

**Length:** Long-form winners are long (20–60 min) on doc channels; our 8.5–10 min is on the short side for the genre but fine for a new channel. Shorts winners are 20–40 s with one idea. Shorts carry 85%+ of views for Tilbury and Kullberg.

**Pacing/editing (from format descriptions, not from viewing):** case-board/evidence visuals (Coffeezilla), 3D reconstruction (fern, Zack D.), prop demos (Yang), two-character skits (Kullberg), slow B-roll + cynical VO (How Money Works).

**CTA style:** could not be observed directly. Known: Kullberg ends on a usable rule ("save this"); Hammer's clips end on the insult; doc channels put the sponsor at ~20–30% and no hard CTA.

**Trending right now (Nov 2026):** gambling/sports-betting exposés; Epstein-money explainers; anything tying a US president to crypto; "new Elizabeth Holmes" framing; "Every X explained in N minutes"; retirement-regret and "what this news means for my money" (OutlierKit claims 23–26× outliers).

**Gaps we can own**
- **Nobody verified here is doing faceless, math-first, 30–45 s Shorts on court-proven frauds** with a single impossible number. Coffeezilla/fern/Magnates are long-form; Yang/Tilbury/Kullberg are on-camera personal finance.
- **Gambling math as arithmetic** (parlay edge, lottery EV) rather than as a moral exposé — Coffeezilla proved the demand; nobody owns the 30-second math version.
- **"The contract/fine-print number"**: payday loans, overdraft APR, BNPL — Kullberg does the consumer-hack side; nobody does "here's the real APR" as a villain story.
- **Sequel framing** ("the new Theranos", "the lottery's own security guy") to borrow search demand from famous cases.
