## One-paragraph answer
Virginia investigated for about three weeks, could not prove the winning ticket broke any rule, and **paid**: 20 yearly cheques of roughly $1.3 million to the fund's trustee, after federal and state tax officials reviewed how to tax foreign winners. The Lottery Board passed **emergency rules against block buying** within weeks. Australian securities regulators looked at the fund itself; the fund was reportedly dissolved after it got its money, and how much each of the ~2,500 investors finally received is **not documented** in anything I could verify. The famous "CIA and FBI investigated him" line appears **only in later retellings**, not in any 1992 report I found. Mandel tried to launch his own lottery in Gibraltar (1993), was reported bankrupt in 1995, and was convicted of securities offences in Israel in 2004 (10 months, in absentia); Israel's Supreme Court refused him a retrial after he returned in 2023. The method itself came back in **Texas in 2023** — and that one ended with the state lottery commission being shut down.

## Story beats
1. **Feb 15, 1992:** the drawing. Within days reporters trace giant block purchases to an Australian group (Roanoke Times, "Australian Lotto Link Revealed", Feb 19).
2. **The snag:** a Virginia rule said tickets must be bought at a licensed retailer. The syndicate's US agent, Illinois businessman **Anithalee Alex Jr.**, had paid for millions of tickets with bank cashier's cheques couriered to grocery-chain head offices. Was the winning ticket "bought at the counter"?
3. **Feb 24–Mar 2:** the Lottery Board first puts off new rules ("ticket-buying mystery deepens", Washington Post, Feb 25), then adopts emergency regulations.
4. **Early March:** the claimants come forward; Stefan Mandel publicly says he is the man behind the scheme (Mar 7–8).
5. **Mar 10, 1992:** Lottery director **Kenneth Thorson** announces the state will pay. His reasoning: nobody could prove how the winning ticket was bought, so the player gets the benefit of the doubt.
6. **Payment:** 20 annual instalments to the fund's trustee, pending a tax review by the IRS and state officials. In 1997 a lottery spokeswoman confirmed the cheques were still going out, with 15 years left.
7. **Rule changes:** stores must serve walk-up customers before bulk orders; retailers may not sell blocks worth more than five times their bond without written permission. UPI: Virginia was the first of 32 lottery states to restrict bulk buying. Thorson said the changes made a repeat unlikely.
8. **Australia:** securities investigators examine whether the International Lotto Fund told its ~2,500 investors about the risks and the taxes. Outcome not found.
9. **Mandel after:** May 1992 the syndicate is reported eyeing Illinois; July 1993 he announces "Globalot", a private lottery based in Gibraltar ($100 tickets, 300,000 max, $10M prize), and US postal officials warn it may be illegal to mail; by 1997 the old syndicate is described as dissolved. Reported bankrupt 1995 (secondary). Israel: 1996 land-in-Vanuatu co-operative → securities conviction 2004, in absentia → returns May 2023 → retrial refused.
10. **Epilogue — it happened again:** April 22, 2023, Lotto Texas. A group called Rook TX bought nearly all 25.8 million combinations and took a $95M jackpot ($57.8M cash). That set off investigations, criminal charges (reported 2026) and the end of the Texas Lottery Commission.

## Convictions
- **For the Virginia lottery: none.** It was legal. Never call it a scam.
- **Stefan Mandel — Israel, 2004:** convicted in absentia of securities-law offences tied to a 1996 investment co-operative; **10 months** and a NIS 100,000 fine. Whether/when he served is **not verified**; older English-language articles (2018–2023) say the conviction was overturned and he "never spent a day behind bars", while others say he served **20 months** — both conflict with ynet's 2023 reporting. **Trust ynet (Israeli outlet, covering the 2023 court ruling); say only "convicted in Israel in 2004 and sentenced to 10 months; in 2023 the Supreme Court refused to reopen the case."**

## Commonly repeated but NOT verifiable — don't say
- "Mandel won the lottery 14 times" as fact — it is his claim (2 in Romania, 12 in Australia, sometimes Virginia counted too); say "says he won 14 times".
- "CIA and FBI investigated" as fact (see table).
- "He served 20 months in an Israeli prison."
- "He lives in Vanuatu today" (Vanuatu is tied to the 1996 land scheme; current residence not confirmed — he was reported in Israel in 2023).
- "Each investor got only $1,400" / "$97,000 in his bank account" — couldn't find a primary source.
- "The team had 72 hours and ran out of time" — the Short already implied time pressure; printing logistics details come from later retellings.
- "Virginia refused to pay for years" / "paid in 2019" — false; decision was March 1992.
- "All 50 states banned it."
- Exact jackpot $27,036,142 and the winning numbers — single-source.
