## Story beats
1. 2016–17: BitConnect launches a coin (BCC) and a "Lending Program": hand over Bitcoin, get BCC, "lend" it to the platform.
2. THE PITCH: a "volatility software trading bot" will earn you up to **40% a month**; the site posted returns averaging about **1% per day**.
3. THE COUNTER: 1% a day compounded is ×37.8 a year. $1,000 → $37,783 (year 1) → $1.43 million (year 2) → $53.9 million (year 3) → past $1 billion on day 1,389 → past the size of the whole world economy in about 7 years. No bot does that. No anything does that.
4. There was no bot trading the money (SEC/DOJ): deposits went to wallets controlled by the founder and promoters; early investors were paid with later investors' money.
5. Recruitment ran like a pyramid: promoters earned commissions on what their recruits put in. US "national promoter" Glenn Arcaro took at least **$24 million**.
6. BCC becomes a top-20 coin; price peaks above $400 in December 2017. October 2017: the Thailand event that produced the "BitConnect!" meme.
7. January 2018: Texas and North Carolina regulators issue cease-and-desist orders. Jan 16–17: the lending platform closes; the coin falls **92%** in a day, then to under $1.
8. Total taken from investors: about **$2.4 billion** (DOJ/SEC).
9. September 2021: Arcaro pleads guilty. September 2022: **38 months**. January 2023: court orders **$17 million** restitution to about 800 victims.
10. The founder, Satish Kumbhani, was indicted in February 2022 — and has not been found. (Alleged.)

## Convictions
- **Glenn Arcaro** (Los Angeles; lead US promoter) — pleaded guilty 2021-09-01 to conspiracy to commit wire fraud; **38 months**, 2022-09-16; forfeiture/restitution as above.
- **Satish Kumbhani** (founder, India) — **indicted only → "alleged"**. A search extract of a later DOJ release says a defendant "remains a fugitive"; that is consistent with Kumbhani, but I could not open the page. Do not say Arcaro is a fugitive.
- BitConnect "the company" — SEC civil suit 2021.
- Not charged: Carlos Matos (meme), most YouTube promoters (some settled with the SEC — not researched).

- "$3.4 billion" / "$4 billion" totals (some crypto sites) — use DOJ's $2.4B.
