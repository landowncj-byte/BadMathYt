# Scott Tucker — "The payday loan that said $650 and took $1,925"
Decoded · Pitch vs Math · compiled 2026-11-24/25

**Access note:** DOJ pages read via search-result extracts (not opened); Wikipedia read directly. ✔2 = two sources; ✔1 = one; ⚠ = verify.

## Story beats
1. Scott Tucker, Kansas businessman — already convicted once (1991, a fake lending company; served a year in Leavenworth) — builds an online payday-loan group from the late 1990s/2001: brands like Ameriloan, 500 FastCash, One Click Cash, United Cash Loans.
2. THE PITCH (the legally required "Truth in Lending" box): borrow $500, total repayment **$650**.
3. THE MATH: by default the loan "auto-renewed". Each payday the lender withdrew only the $150 finance charge and left the $500 principal untouched — then did it again. DOJ's example borrower paid **$1,925**: $1,425 in charges on a $500 loan.
4. Scale: at least **4.5 million** borrowers; **over $3.5 billion** in revenue from 2008 to June 2013; rates of 600–700% a year, sometimes above 1,000%.
5. The shield: when states came after him, Tucker claimed the businesses were owned by Native American tribes (which are immune from state suits). Prosecutors called the tribal ownership a sham; the tribes got a small cut.
6. Where the money went: Level 5 Motorsports — his own professional sports-car racing team — Ferraris, a private jet, a house in Aspen (lifestyle list per DOJ/press; verify items before listing).
7. April 2012: FTC sues. February 2016: arrested. **October 13, 2017: convicted on all 14 counts** with his lawyer Timothy Muir.
8. **January 5, 2018: 200 months (16 years 8 months).** Muir: 84 months.
9. Aftermath: FTC mailed ~1.2 million refund checks totalling $505 million (Sept 2018). In 2021 the Supreme Court (AMG Capital Management v. FTC, 9–0) ruled the FTC never had the power to win that kind of money judgment — a case that carries his company's name.

## Convictions
- **Scott Tucker** — convicted by jury 2017-10-13 (SDNY) on 14 counts incl. racketeering, wire fraud, money laundering, TILA offenses. **200 months**, 2018. Earlier: 1991 conviction (mail fraud, false statements to a bank; ~1 year served).
- **Timothy Muir** (his attorney) — same 14 counts; **84 months**.
- Joel Tucker (brother) — separate case; pleaded guilty 2020 (stolen money, bankruptcy fraud, tax evasion) per Wikipedia.

## Commonly repeated but NOT verified here — don't say
- "He stole $2 billion" (2016 arrest headlines) vs "$3.5 billion" (trial) — $3.5B is *revenue*, not theft. Say "a $3.5 billion business".
- "Victims got all their money back" — only $505M was distributed.
- FTC example "$300 loan → $975" — real FTC figure as I recall, but not confirmed this session.
- The Netflix *Dirty Money* episode details (the "Payday" episode) — don't cite from memory.
- "The tribes were in on it / innocent" — two tribal corporations reached non-prosecution agreements with forfeiture (not verified here); leave tribes as "he claimed tribes owned it; a jury found that was a sham".
