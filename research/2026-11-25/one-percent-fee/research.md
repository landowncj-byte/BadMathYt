# The 1% fee — "It's only 1%… of everything, every year"
What If · Dilemma · pure arithmetic · compiled 2026-11-24/25

## The claim in one line
Two identical funds, one charges 1% a year more. Over 40 years of $500 a month, the "1%" takes 24% of the final pot: $316,661.

## Assumptions (on screen)
- Invest **$500 a month for 40 years** ($240,000 put in). Hypothetical.
- Fund A earns **7% a year** before fees and charges ~0. Fund B is identical but charges **1% a year**, so it nets **6%**.
- Monthly compounding, constant return, no taxes, no inflation adjustment.
- 7% is an assumption, not a forecast or a promise. No real fund is named.

## Formula
Future value of monthly deposits: FV = P × [((1 + r/12)^(12×years) − 1) ÷ (r/12)], P = $500.

## Commonly repeated but NOT verifiable — don't say
- "The average fund charges 1%" — false today; asset-weighted average fund fees are well under 0.5% (Morningstar/ICI publish this; not opened here). Say "some funds and advisers still charge around 1%".
- "A 1% fee eats a third / half of your returns" — depends on horizon and whether you count the pot or the gains; our stated number is 24.1% of the final pot at 40 years (and 29.5% of the gains: 316,661 ÷ 1,072,407).
- "Index funds always win" — advice + not always true.
- "Advisers are scammers."
