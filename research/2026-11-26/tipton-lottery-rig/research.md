# Eddie Tipton / Hot Lotto — "The lottery's own security chief rigged the lottery"
Decoded · Case File · compiled 2026-11-24/25

**Access note:** main source is Wikipedia's "Hot Lotto fraud scandal" (read directly) plus news extracts via search (CNN Money, CBS, The Gazette, NBC). ✔2 / ✔1 / ⚠ as elsewhere.

**Primary re-check 2026-11-25 (second pass):** this was an **Iowa state** prosecution (Iowa Attorney General / Polk County court), so there is no DOJ release; the Iowa AG and Iowa court sites were not on the allowed list. ## Story beats
1. Eddie Tipton is **information security director** of the Multi-State Lottery Association (MUSL) in Iowa — the office that runs the computers that draw numbers for games in many states. He helped write the random number generator (RNG) software.
2. Around 2005–06 he hides extra code in it: if a drawing falls on **one of three dates** (May 27, Nov 23, Dec 29), on a Wednesday or Saturday, after 8 p.m., the "random" numbers become predictable to him.
3. He can't play — lottery employees are banned by law — so he feeds numbers to others: his brother Tommy (a Texas justice of the peace) and friend Robert Rhodes. Wins: Colorado 2005 ($568,990 share), Wisconsin 2007 ($783,257), Oklahoma 2011 ($1.2M), plus Kansas.
4. December 2010: the big one. A hooded man buys a Hot Lotto ticket — and hot dogs — at a Des Moines QuikTrip. On **December 29, 2010** it hits a jackpot advertised at **$16.5 million**.
5. The problem: claiming it means showing ID. For a year nobody comes forward.
6. Then the attempts: a man in Quebec phones with the ticket's serial code but can't answer basic questions; a Belize shell trust ("Hexham Investments") tries; **less than two hours before the one-year deadline**, lawyers hand in the ticket — with the trust's name misspelled. Iowa doesn't pay anonymous claims. Rejected.
7. October 2014: investigators release the store video. Co-workers recognise the voice: it's the lottery's own security chief. Arrested January 2015.
8. Forensics on an old Wisconsin machine finds the date-trigger code.
9. June 2017: Tipton pleads guilty and tells the court he wrote code to predict the numbers. **August 22, 2017: 25 years.** Paroled July 2022.
10. Payoff: total verified take from the earlier rigs was a couple of million; the $16.5M — the one that would have made him rich — was never paid to anyone.

## Convictions
- **Eddie Raymond Tipton** — pleaded guilty June 2017 (Iowa: ongoing criminal conduct; also Wisconsin theft by fraud and computer crime). **25 years**, 2017-08-22. Paroled 2022. His 2015 jury convictions on the Iowa ticket were overturned on time-limit grounds in 2017 — so say "pleaded guilty to rigging lotteries in five states", not "convicted of the $16.5M ticket".
- **Tommy Tipton** (brother) — pleaded guilty 2017 (conspiracy to commit theft by deception); 75 days in jail.
- **Robert Rhodes** — pleaded guilty 2017; 6 months house arrest; $409,000 restitution.
- In 2020 Tipton sued claiming he was pressured into the plea (Wikipedia) — mention only as "he later tried to take it back".

## On-screen-safe quotes
- Eddie Tipton in court, June 2017: **"I wrote software that included code that allowed me to technically predict winning numbers and I gave those numbers to other individuals who then won the lottery and shared those winnings with me."** — as quoted on Wikipedia (Hot Lotto fraud scandal, "Confession and second conviction"). Wikipedia cites press coverage of the plea hearing; confirm against the AP/Des Moines Register report before it goes on screen.
- No other verified quotes. (The judge cited his "greed" — one word, CNN Money.)

## Commonly repeated but NOT verified here — don't say
- "He bought the ticket on December 23, 2010" — date widely repeated; not confirmed in sources I could read.
- "He stole $24 million" / "the biggest lottery scam in US history: $X" — totals vary by outlet; stick to itemised wins.
- "He used a USB stick with a self-deleting rootkit" — that was the *prosecution's theory* at the 2015 trial; the proven mechanism (his plea + forensics) is date-trigger code in the RNG software. Say "prosecutors believed".
- "He won $16.5 million" — nobody collected it.
- "Hot dogs gave him away" — the hot dogs are in the video (CBS); the voice identified him.
- "24 rigged drawings" — not found.
