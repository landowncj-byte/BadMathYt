## Story beats
1. 2014, Sofia, Bulgaria: **OneCoin** launches as "the Bitcoin killer", sold through a multi-level marketing network — you buy "trader packages", you recruit, you earn commission.
2. It spreads to more than **3.5 million** people worldwide. They put in more than **$4 billion**.
3. Members log into a website and watch their coins go up in value. Always up.
4. The tell: a real cryptocurrency has a **public blockchain** anyone can check. Prosecutors said OneCoin had **no true blockchain** — no public, verifiable ledger. The price wasn't set by a market; **the company set it**.
5. It gets worse: by about March 2015, prosecutors said, the founders were handing members coins that didn't exist even in the company's own private ledger. Internally they called them **"fake coins"**.
6. The money: the US co-founder and top seller, **Karl Sebastian Greenwood**, was making about **€20 million a month**.
7. October 2017: the other co-founder, Ruja Ignatova — the "Cryptoqueen" — boards a flight from Sofia to Athens and is never seen again. She is charged, not convicted.
8. Greenwood is arrested (2018), **pleads guilty (December 2022)**, and on **September 12, 2023** gets **20 years** and a **$300 million** forfeiture order.
9. Payoff: $4 billion went into a "coin" whose entire existence was a number on one company's website.

## Story loop (suggested)
STAKES: 3.5 million people, $4 billion, your aunt's group chat. · QUESTION: how do you check whether a crypto coin is real? · TWIST: you look at its blockchain — and this one didn't have a real one. The "price" was typed in by the people selling it. · REHOOKS: "the price only ever went up" → "they were handing out coins that weren't even in their own system" → "one founder made €20M a month" → "the other one vanished".

## Commonly repeated but NOT verified here — don't say
- "No blockchain at all / it was an Excel spreadsheet / an SQL database" — the documented claim is "no true (public, verifiable) blockchain". Yesterday's seed line "a coin that only existed in one company's spreadsheet" must become "…on one company's own servers" or "…a number on a website".
- The "take the money and run and blame someone else" exit-strategy email — widely attributed to the 2019 SDNY release; **could not be confirmed in any extract today**. No quote.
- "$4.4 billion", "$4.5 billion", "$15 billion", "£4 billion" — outlets vary; use "more than $4 billion".
- "Ignatova was murdered on a yacht in 2018" / "she had plastic surgery" / "she's in Dubai / South Africa" — press speculation.
- "The biggest crypto scam in history" as flat fact → the State Department's phrase is "one of the largest global fraud schemes in history" (✔1; paraphrase).
- "It was a Ponzi scheme" — DOJ's terms are "fraud scheme" and "pyramid scheme"; press says Ponzi. Use "pyramid scheme".
- Never mock the 3.5 million buyers. The roast target is the product ("a crypto coin with no real crypto") and the convicted co-founder.
