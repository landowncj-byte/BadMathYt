### 10. $25,000 Cash or One Roll of a Die for $1,000,000?
- **Series / format:** What If · Dilemma (sure thing vs gamble)
- **Hook fact (exact math):** the roll is worth **$166,667 on average** — 6.7× the sure cash — and you walk away with nothing **5 times out of 6**. Coin-flip cousin: $50,000 sure vs 50% at $1M has an average of $500,000 (10×).
- **Question / twist:** "the math says roll" — but with the standard log-utility model the roll only makes sense if you already have about **$31,800** to your name (solve ln(W+25,000) = ⅙·ln(W+1,000,000) + ⅚·ln(W) → W ≈ $31,816; for the coin-flip version the closed form is W = $2,777.78). Being broke changes the right answer.
- **Why 18–28s care:** it's the viral "guaranteed vs gamble" argument with a number that tells *them* which side they're on.

### 11. The $20 Bill That Sells for More Than $20
- **Series / format:** What If · Myth vs Math (the "dollar auction")
- **Hook fact:** in economist Martin Shubik's 1971 "dollar auction" the top bidder wins the bill but the **second-highest bidder also has to pay** — so once two people are in, quitting always costs more than bidding one more dollar, and the bill routinely sells for more than its face value.
- **Twist:** at $19 vs $20 the loser bids $21 for a $20 bill — to lose $1 instead of $19.
- **Status / weakness:** the mechanism is solid (Shubik 1971; Wikipedia "Dollar auction"; Kellogg paper). The famous anecdotes — a professor "earned $17,000 selling $20 bills to MBA students", "record bid $204", "top two bids once totalled $407" — were found **only in blogs/forums**, with the school given as both Harvard and Northwestern. **Do not voice them** until Bazerman's own book (*Judgment in Managerial Decision Making*) or Murnighan (2002, *J. of Management Education* 26:56–69) is opened.
