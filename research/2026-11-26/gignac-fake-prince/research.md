## Story beats
1. Miami, 2015–2017: a man calling himself **Prince Khalid Bin Al-Saud** of the Saudi royal family lives on Fisher Island with a sign reading "Sultan" on the door.
2. The props: a Ferrari with **diplomatic licence plates**, bodyguards carrying **Diplomatic Security Service badges**, jewellery, royal-protocol demands (gifts expected before any meeting).
3. All fake. The plates and badges were bought; he was **Anthony Gignac**, raised in Michigan, with no royal blood and no diplomatic status.
4. The pitch: he could invest hundreds of millions — including in a famous Miami Beach hotel — and he had exclusive deals investors could get into. Investors wired him **more than $8 million**.
5. The tell: at a dinner, a would-be business partner watched the "devout Saudi royal" order **pork**. The partner's security team started digging.
6. November 2017: arrested. 2018: pleads guilty. **May 31, 2019: more than 18 years in federal prison.**
7. The self-own twist: prosecutors said that since **1988** he'd been arrested or convicted **11 times** for the *same* prince act. He kept getting out and putting the costume back on.

## Story loop (suggested)
STAKES: real investors, $8 million, a man with diplomatic plates. · QUESTION: how do you check whether a prince is a prince? · TWIST: nobody checked the plates or the badge. What cracked it was his dinner order. · REHOOKS: "the plates were fake" → "the bodyguards' badges were fake" → "then he ordered the pork" → "and this was arrest number eleven".
