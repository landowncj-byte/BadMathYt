## What the 8 have in common (use these, not the topics)
1. **The viewer answers before second 2.** Every dilemma outlier shows both options at frame 0 as text/objects. → Our frame 0 for #1, #3, #6 must show A and B *already on screen* with the amounts, not build to them.
2. **A hidden number flips the "obvious" answer.** 8.33 years (MrBeast), $500K average (coin flip), 20 years (the $1K/week winner). → Each of our dilemmas names its flip number: **2.56 lb per inch**, **2 in 3**, **day 27**.
3. **Design for a split, not a right answer.** The best ones sit near 50/50 so both camps comment. → Weight-vs-height is naturally split by body type; Monty Hall splits by intuition; penny-doubling is one-sided, so its hook must attack the *winning* side ("you'd have quit on day 20").
4. **A real person or a real pile-on as the rehook.** 10,000 letters; a billionaire dunking on a 20-year-old; 16,000 poll answers. → Use the vos Savant letters in #3; use poll-style "most people pick B" lines only where we have the data (we don't for #1 — don't invent a percentage).
5. **Tiny-detail downfall for cons.** The title/hook promises one small physical slip. → #2 (a date on a label), #5 (a dinner order), #4 (a ticket he couldn't cash).
6. **Ask = the choice itself.** None of the dilemma outliers needs a pitch CTA; the question is the engagement. Matches rules 46–53: our comment question after the payoff should simply be the A/B, in one line.
7. **Pacing:** one idea, one object per beat, turn by ~second 5 ("but…", "wrong", "that's not what got him"). This is exactly the seconds-3–8 gap ANALYTICS flagged.
