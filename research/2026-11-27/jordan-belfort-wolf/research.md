# Jordan Belfort — "The real Wolf of Wall Street got 22 months. His victims lost $110 million."

## Story beats
1. **Late 1980s–90s, New York/Long Island:** Jordan Belfort founds **Stratton Oakmont**, a brokerage that cold-calls ordinary people and sells them **penny stocks** (very cheap shares of tiny companies).
2. The trick = **"pump and dump"**: insiders secretly hold the cheap shares, the salesmen pump the price with calls, then insiders dump at the high price and the price collapses on the customers. (NPR: Belfort and partners secretly held shares.)
3. Meanwhile the parties: the movie version (don't claim the movie's details as fact).
4. **1996:** the industry regulator **expels Stratton Oakmont** (Dec 1996). FBI agent **Gregory Coleman** had been chasing the firm since **1992**.
5. **1998–99:** arrested; indicted/pleads guilty (1999) to **securities fraud and money laundering**. The tell: **he talked.** Belfort cooperated and **wore a recording device** on former colleagues (✔2).
6. **2003:** sentenced to **4 years**; served about **22 months** (Taft prison); ordered to pay about **$110 million** in restitution.
7. Payoff: years later reports said he'd repaid only a fraction and prosecutors/CBS reported he "must still pay back nearly $100 million" (CBS 2018).

## Story loop
STAKES: a boiler room that made young guys rich by emptying strangers' savings. · QUESTION: how do you get caught when everyone's partying? · TWIST: the king of the firm became the FBI's informant. · REHOOKS: "penny stocks" → "pump and dump" → "the wire" → "22 months".

## Convictions
- **Jordan Belfort** — pleaded guilty 1999 (securities fraud, money laundering); sentenced 2003, 4 years, served 22 months; restitution about $110M.
- **Danny Porush** (partner, the movie's "Donnie") — about 39 months/4 years per extracts ⚠; **don't name** unless verified.
