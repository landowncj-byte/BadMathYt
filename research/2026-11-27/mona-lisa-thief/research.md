# Vincenzo Peruggia — "He stole the Mona Lisa. Then he wrote a shop to ask for money."
Decoded · Heist (the world's most famous painting) · compiled 2026-11-26 for Fri 2026-11-27

**Access note:** Wikipedia/justice.gov fetches fail in this sandbox (host not allowed). Everything read through WebSearch extracts. Grades: ✔2 = two independent outlets agree · ✔1 = one outlet · ⚠ = outlets conflict / weak. Re-open a source before any on-screen quote.

## Story beats
1. **Monday 21 August 1911, Paris:** the Louvre is closed. Vincenzo Peruggia, an Italian who had worked at the Louvre, walks in early and walks out with the Mona Lisa under his arm. (Time of day "~7 am" ✔1.)
2. Next day a painter notices the empty hooks. The Louvre closes for a week; newspapers worldwide print the empty wall. Crowds queue to stare at the blank spot.
3. For **over two years** nobody finds it. It sits in Peruggia's small Paris room (trunk story ✔1 — say "his flat").
4. **Nov 1913:** the tell. He gets greedy/lonely for a payday and **writes to a Florence art dealer, Alfredo Geri**, offering the painting for **500,000 lire** and saying it should "go home to Italy".
5. Geri calls the director of the Uffizi gallery, **Giovanni Poggi**. They meet Peruggia at his hotel, look at the painting, confirm it is real, and tell him they'll "take it to be checked". **Dec 11/12, 1913: police arrest him** at his hotel. (date ⚠ 11 vs 12 → say "mid-December 1913".)
6. **Jan 1914:** the painting goes back to Paris. 
7. **Trial, Florence, mid-1914:** he says he did it for Italy. Court gives **1 year and 15 days**; on appeal it's cut and he's freed having served about **seven months** (served time ⚠ 6 / 7 / 7m8d). The patriotic claim made the sentence light.
8. Payoff: the theft turned a famous painting into THE most famous painting on Earth — and the thief was caught by a dealer he wrote to himself.

## Story loop (suggested)
STAKES: the most famous painting on Earth walks out of the Louvre in broad daylight. · QUESTION: how do you catch a thief when there's no camera, no fingerprints that work, no clue? · TWIST: nobody caught him. He wrote to a shop and asked for money. · REHOOKS: "closed Monday" → "two years, nothing" → "one letter, five hundred thousand lire" → "arrested at his hotel".

## Convictions
- **Vincenzo Peruggia** — convicted at a Florence trial in 1914, sentence 1 year 15 days (served about 7 months). Confessed; claimed patriotism. "Convicted thief" and "sentenced" are safe. Nobody else was convicted; the "mastermind forger / Argentine con man Valfierno" story is a 1932 newspaper tale, NOT court-proven — do not tell it.

## Don't say
- That he was a "janitor in a smock who hid in a broom closet" as fact — closet/smock versions are ⚠ (one source flags the smock story as doubtful). Safe: "walked out with it".
- That his fingerprint "should have caught him" — fingerprint lore is not confirmed in extracts. Cut.
- "Valfierno" 6-copies con (secondary legend). Cut.
- Exact arrest day (11 vs 12 Dec) → "mid-December 1913".
- "He served six months / seven months" as exact → "under a year".
- A mocking tone about Italians; he is the roast target, not a nation.
