# "Count" Victor Lustig — "He sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice."
Decoded · Classic con (the most famous con line in history) · compiled 2026-11-26 for Fri 2026-11-27

**Access note:** pages unreadable here; WebSearch extracts only. **IMPORTANT:** the Eiffel Tower sale rests on Lustig's own memoir-style legend and later retellings. Smithsonian and others tell it as "claimed/reportedly". The **court record** (counterfeiting, Alcatraz) is the verified half. Tell the Tower as "the story goes" and the conviction as fact.

## Story beats
1. **Paris, 1925 (story):** Lustig, a Czech-born con man using fake titles ("Count"), reads that the Eiffel Tower is costly to maintain.
2. He poses as a French government official, invites **five scrap-metal dealers** to a luxury hotel, says the government will secretly sell the tower for scrap, and picks the most eager one, **André Poisson** (✔2 on name via Smithsonian/Mental Floss).
3. Poisson pays; Lustig leaves for Vienna with the cash (amount ⚠).
4. The tell: Poisson was **too embarrassed to go to the police**. Lustig read the papers — nothing. So he **came back and did it again** with a new batch of dealers; this time a victim reported it and he fled.
5. **US 1930s:** he sells the "Romanian money box" (a box that "prints" $100 bills) to rich marks, then runs a real counterfeiting ring.
6. **May 1935:** arrested in New York by Secret Service (agent Peter Rubano ✔1). He **escapes** a federal detention center by rope (**Sep 1935**) and is recaptured weeks later.
7. **Plea/sentence:** 15 years for counterfeiting + 5 for the escape = **20 years at Alcatraz** (sources split 15 vs 20; see grades).
8. He died in prison hospital in **March 1947** (pneumonia; death certificate: Medical Center for Federal Prisoners, Springfield, Missouri, 11 Mar 1947 ✔ Commons PD record). His death certificate reportedly listed occupation "apprentice salesman" ⚠ — **don't say**.

## Story loop
STAKES: a landmark that belongs to the whole country, for sale in a hotel room. · QUESTION: how do you sell something nobody can own? · TWIST: his entire trick was picking the one buyer too proud to call the police. · REHOOKS: "five scrap dealers" → "Poisson says yes" → "silence…so he came back" → "twenty years".

## Convictions
- **Victor Lustig** — pleaded guilty to counterfeiting 1935 (NY federal court); 15 years + 5 for escape (20 total) ✔2; **he was never convicted for the Eiffel Tower** (never charged for it). Say "convicted of counterfeiting".
- **Billy Watts** (partner turned witness) — received 10 years per two sources ✔2. Optional.

## Don't say
- That the Eiffel Tower sale is court-proven. It is not.
- "Sold it twice for $X" (amounts unknown).
- The Capone story as fact.
- "Died on Alcatraz" or "apprentice salesman".
- Ten Commandments of con men (lore, attributed to him; fun but ⚠). Could be a loop line only if marked "he supposedly kept a list" — skip.
- Real French officials/people beyond Poisson (surname only, "a scrap dealer").
