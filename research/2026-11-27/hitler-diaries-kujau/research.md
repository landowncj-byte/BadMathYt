# Konrad Kujau — "A magazine paid nine million marks for Hitler's diaries. The covers said 'FH'."
Decoded · Fake-document con (historic hoax) · compiled 2026-11-26 for Fri 2026-11-27

**Access note:** Wikipedia/Britannica pages unreadable; WebSearch extracts only. Sentence figures vary by source — see grades. Sensitive subject: keep Hitler imagery off-screen (no portraits/symbols); the roast target is the forger and the magazine's rush.

## Story beats
1. **Early 1980s, West Germany:** a Stuttgart dealer in Nazi memorabilia, **Konrad Kujau**, writes dozens of handwritten "diaries of Adolf Hitler" (1981–83) and, using the name "Konrad Fischer", sells them through a go-between.
2. A star reporter at **Stern**, a big German news magazine, **Gerd Heidemann**, believes them and persuades his bosses to pay about **9 million Deutsche marks** (sources say 9.0 to 9.3 million). Heidemann secretly skims a large part of the money.
3. **April 1983:** Stern announces the find to the world; newspapers abroad bid for rights.
4. The tell: **the covers had the initials "FH" in Gothic letters — not "AH".** Kujau had bought plastic letters in a department store (made in Hong Kong per Wikipedia ✔1; Britannica ✔1 says he was confused by the Gothic "A" and put "F"). 
5. Less than two weeks later, West Germany's **Federal Archives** tests the books and calls them a "crude forgery": paper with a whitener not used until the 1950s, modern ink (accounts differ on details).
6. Kujau and Heidemann are arrested. **July 1985, Hamburg:** Kujau gets about **4½ years**, Heidemann **4 years 8 months** for fraud/forgery/embezzlement.
7. Payoff: experts had been "sure" — the one thing nobody did before paying was run a cheap paper test. The giveaway was two letters on the front.

## Story loop
STAKES: "Hitler's secret diaries," the scoop of the century, millions of marks. · QUESTION: how do you fake something world-famous experts can't spot? · TWIST: he got the first letter of Hitler's name wrong on the cover. · REHOOKS: "nine million marks" → "the covers said FH" → "tested after printing" → "four and a half years".

## Convictions
- **Konrad Kujau** — convicted of forgery/fraud, Hamburg court, July 1985, about 4½ years ✔2.
- **Gerd Heidemann** — convicted (fraud/embezzlement), same trial, about 4 yrs 8 mo ✔2.
- **Stern** — victim; two editors resigned (✔1, skip). Don't say it was "in on it".

## Don't say
- "Polyester thread" / "60 volumes" / "He fooled historian Hugh Trevor-Roper" (extract didn't confirm; widely reported but not opened).
- Exact Stern headline, Newsweek, Sunday Times details.
- Any sympathetic or humorous treatment of Hitler himself.
- Kujau's later life (painting "originals", TV shows) — not checked.
