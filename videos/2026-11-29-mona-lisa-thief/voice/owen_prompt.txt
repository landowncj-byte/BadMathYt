[dramatically] A man walked the Mona Lisa out under his arm. Who was he? Vincenzo Peruggia, and he used to work at the Louvre. August twenty-first, nineteen eleven. The museum was closed, and he knew it. He took the painting off the wall and walked straight out. The next day, a painter noticed four empty hooks where the Mona Lisa should be. [playfully] Hit like if you'd have spotted it faster. [matter of fact] Then the Louvre closed for a week, and newspapers worldwide printed the empty wall. And the police? They had nothing. No suspect, no painting, for over two years. Because it was hidden in his small Paris apartment. So how did they catch him? He caught himself. In nineteen thirteen, he wrote a letter to an art dealer in Florence. He offered the Mona Lisa for five hundred thousand lire, and said it should go home to Italy. [playfully] Send this to the friend who'd brag too. [curious] The dealer brought the Uffizi gallery's director. And at his hotel, what did they find? [conversational] It was the real one. They said they'd take it to be checked, but then the police came. [playfully] Follow if you love when the bad guy trips over himself. [seriously] In nineteen fourteen, a court in Florence sentenced him to one year and fifteen days in prison. He said he did it for Italy, and served less than a year. And the theft? It made the Mona Lisa the most famous painting in the world. [playfully] Two years with no clue, then one letter. Would you have mailed it?
