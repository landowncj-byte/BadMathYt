## ASSUMES (What If: pinned chip from the first briefcase beat to the end, plus the HYPOTHETICAL chip)
`ASSUMES: exactly one case holds $1,000,000, placed at random · THE HOST KNOWS where it is · he ALWAYS opens an empty case you didn't pick and ALWAYS offers the swap` — the host-knows rule is also SPOKEN in every cut (if he opened a case at random the answer would be 50/50).
Math (exactly as research.md): stay = 1/3 (33.33%) · switch = 2/3 (66.67%) · switch is exactly 2× · 100-case version: he opens 98 empties, switching wins 99/100 · average payout $333,333 vs $666,667 (on screen only).
True-story lines (✔2, attributed): magazine column, September 1990; "by her count" nearly 1,000 letters carried PhD signatures (≈10,000 letters in all, most disagreeing); the real game-show host tried it himself and switching won (paraphrase, no quote).
Not used on purpose: "world's smartest woman / IQ 228", "92% of readers", the Erdős anecdote, any letter quote, doors/car/goats, the host's name or likeness, calling the letter-writers dumb (they 'got it wrong', as fact).
SAY layer: PhDs = "P H Ds".
