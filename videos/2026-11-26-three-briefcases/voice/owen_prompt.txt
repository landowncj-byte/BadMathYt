For twenty-seven years. You pick a case. But the host knows where the money is. And he always opens an empty case you didn't pick. [playfully] Then he asks: stay with yours, or switch? Hit like if it feels fifty-fifty. [conversational] A columnist printed the answer in nineteen ninety: switch. By her count, nearly a thousand PhDs wrote in to say she was wrong. Let's check every— every possible start. [matter of fact] One: you picked the money. The other case is empty, so switching loses. [playfully] Send this to the friend who'd stop listening now. [matter of fact] Two: you picked an empty case. So he has to open the other empty one, and switching wins. Three: you picked the other empty case, so switching wins again. [excited] That's two wins out of three. [curious] So switching literally doubles your chances. Wild, right? [playfully] Follow, this page settles arguments. [conversational] Make it a hundred cases.
