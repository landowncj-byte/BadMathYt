## ASSUMES (What If: pinned chip from the first briefcase beat to the end, plus the HYPOTHETICAL chip)
`ASSUMES: exactly one case holds $1,000,000, placed at random · THE HOST KNOWS where it is · he ALWAYS opens an empty case you didn't pick and ALWAYS offers the swap`. The host-knows + always rule is SPOKEN in every cut.
Math (as research/2026-11-25/monty-hall-briefcases/research.md): stay = 1/3 · switch = 2/3 · three possible starts (money / empty A / empty B): switching loses 1, wins 2 · 100 cases, host opens 98 empties: first pick right 1/100, switching wins 99/100 = 99%.
True-story lines (✔2, attributed "by her count"): a columnist printed the answer ("switch") in 1990; nearly 1,000 letters carried PhD signatures and said she was wrong.
## Everyday comparison (our own arithmetic, no outside input)
$1,000,000 ÷ $100 per day = 10,000 days; 10,000 ÷ 365.25 = 27.4 years → spoken "a hundred bucks a day. For twenty-seven years." (two sentences, one number each). On screen: "$100 A DAY × 10,000 DAYS ≈ 27 YEARS · OUR MATH".
## Kindergarten check
Who/what before the trick: three cases, one has the money, you pick, the host KNOWS and ALWAYS opens an empty one, then offers stay or switch. The three possible first picks are walked one at a time; the key number (two out of three) is said once before the 100-case aha. "Stay" is explained as staying with your case.
