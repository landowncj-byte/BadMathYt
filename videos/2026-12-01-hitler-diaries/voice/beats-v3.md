Facts (research/2026-11-27/hitler-diaries-kujau, ✔2 unless noted): early 1980s West Germany; Konrad Kujau, a Stuttgart dealer in Nazi-era memorabilia, hand-wrote fake "Hitler diaries" and sold them under a fake name through a go-between; Stern reporter Gerd Heidemann believed them and got his bosses to pay about 9 million marks; Heidemann secretly kept a big chunk (✔1 wording, no figure); April 1983 Stern announced the find; covers carried plastic initials "FH" instead of "AH", from a shop; Germany's Federal Archives found modern paper and ink and called it a crude forgery less than two weeks after the announcement; the real tests came after publication (✔1); July 1985 Hamburg court: about four and a half years each. Avoided: volume count, Trevor-Roper, polyester thread, Hong Kong, Kujau's later life, any Hitler imagery/praise; neutral tone, roast target = the forger and the rush.
