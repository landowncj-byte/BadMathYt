**Version 3, 2026-11-26.** Version 2 is kept as `STORYBOARD.v2.md`. The demo built from the earlier board was rejected by the owner:

## 0. The laws of version 3

1. **SAY IT → SHOW IT.** Each clause gets its own shot, and the shot shows exactly that clause. If a viewer cannot name the action in 3 words, the shot is redone. The 3-word name is the first thing in every WATCH cell, in bold.
2. **FOUR TESTS, as columns.** WATCH (a muted viewer), HEAR (the words), COLOR (what the colours say), SOUND (the effect and the music). All four must say the same thing. A fifth column, DENSITY, lists what moves and what new information the shot adds.
3. **CAST LOCK.** One colour and one costume per character for the whole video; a name card on first appearance (table in §2).
4. **BIG AND CLEAR.** Medium shots and close-ups by default. A wide only to establish a new place, at most 1.5 s. At most 3 things to read. Main characters are never small.
5. **CUT FAST, STAY DENSE.** Average shot 1.1–1.4 s. Nothing over 2.5 s. Every shot has one main action that matches the words plus 1–2 supporting moving parts. Nothing is static.
6. **CONTEXT ALWAYS RECOVERABLE.** Who (name cards, costume), where (a 1–3 word place label on every new set), when (calendar, newspaper).
7. **NUMBERS ON PROPS.** Every spoken number is on a physical prop in the shot where it is spoken.
8. **FACTS.** Nothing is staged as fact that `research/2026-11-26/kurniawan-fake-wine/research.md` does not support (flags in §5).
9. Kept from version 2 wherever it does not fight law 1: the con owns the left, six recurring symbols, physical number props (year rack, coin tower), eyewitness cameras, snap faces.

### The mute story (WATCH column in 8 sentences)
1. Rich people in top hats hand piles of cash across a counter for one dusty bottle. A man in a red suit watches. At home, in his kitchen, the red man fills that bottle from a jug, corks it and sticks a label on it.
2. He is Rudy, a wine collector from California.
3. At an auction, rich people raise paddles against each other until a tower of gold coins stands next to one bottle. In 2006 his crates sell for 35 million dollars. That is a bundle a day until you are a very old person. He gets a world-record ribbon.
4. In New York more of his bottles go on sale. One label says 1945.
5. In France the real winemaker, in green, sees that label and phones the auction, furious. His rack of years is empty until 1982, where the first bottle sits. The label is 37 years older than the wine: red cross. The bottle is pulled from the sale and the red man sweats.
6. In 2012 the FBI comes through his front door and handcuffs him. His kitchen is full of bottles, corks, wax and labels.
7. Three steps: mix an old jug and a new jug, pour the mix into old bottles, new cork and wax, slap on a label. A drawer bursts with thousands of labels.
8. In court a sad gold billionaire shows what he paid, 2.1 million, for bottles marked with red crosses. The jury says guilty. Ten years behind bars. He must pay back 28.4 million. Would you have paid?

## 3. How to read the scene tables

- **WATCH** starts with the 3-word action in bold, then what a muted viewer sees.
- **HEAR** = the exact words spoken during the shot.
- **DENSITY** = `Moves:` the main action + supporting moving parts · `New:` the information this shot adds.
- **COLOR** = what the colours tell (dictionary in §1).
- **SOUND** = effect with its cue (`@word` = start of that word) · `M:` music state.
- **STAGING** = shot size · observer type · faces (bible §2.4, `→` is a snap) · camera.
- **NEW** = anything the engine cannot do today (see §6). `WIP` = present but uncommitted and unverified in `looks.js` / `actions.js`. `—` = everything exists.
- All cuts are hard cuts unless STAGING says otherwise. Captions run under every shot.
