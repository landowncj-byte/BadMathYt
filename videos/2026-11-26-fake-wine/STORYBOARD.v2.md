### The mute story (what the pictures alone must say)
1. Rich people throw piles of money at one dusty old bottle. A man with a red hanky is behind it. He filled that bottle himself, in a kitchen at home.
2. Who is he? A real man called Rudy, a wine collector in California.
3. At an auction, rich people out-bid each other until a tower of coins stands next to one bottle.
4. In 2006 he sold his bottles for 35 million dollars. That is a bundle of cash every day until you are an old man with a beard to the floor. He even got a world-record ribbon.
5. In New York more of his bottles go on sale. One label says 1945.
6. The real winemaker bursts in. He unfolds a rack of years: every slot is empty until 1982, where the first bottle sits. The label is 37 years older than the wine. The con sweats; the bottle is pulled from the sale.
7. Agents knock on his door at night and handcuff him. Their torch finds the kitchen from the start: bottles, corks, wax, labels. Three steps: mix old and new wine, pour it into an old bottle with a new cork and wax, stick on a label. A drawer bursts with thousands of labels.
8. In court a rich man holds a cheque for 2.1 million dollars and points at the bottles. The jury stands: guilty. Ten years behind bars. The pile of money slides away from him: 28.4 million to pay back. The coin host holds up the bottle and asks us a question, and the gavel is in the air again.

### Screen geography (rule S9; never broken)
- **The con owns the LEFT of the frame.** He enters from the left, stands on the left, and his kitchen door is on the left.
- **Buyers, the truth and the law come from the RIGHT:** bidders, the winemaker, the agents, the witness, the jury.
- **Money moving right → left = he is winning** (scenes 1, 5). **Money moving left → right = he is paying** (15.5). The same pile makes both trips.
- The camera never crosses that line inside a scene. An orbit stays on the audience's side of the room.

### Director flags to resolve before the build

It shows one sourced fact only: that wine was first made in 1982, so no bottle of it exists for 1945–1981 (37 empty slots: 1945 through 1981). It must not show a winery name, and the empty slots must not suggest the family made no wine at all before 1982; the rack is "this one wine, year by year". 7. **14.3 — red evidence tags on the bottles** mean "the witness said these are fake" (tape tag `HE SAID: FAKE`), and they appear only inside the court sketch.
8. **15.2 — the stamp reads `GUILTY · FRAUD`** (plain words for "convicted him of fraud"). Confirm the wording against research.md; fallback `CONVICTED · FRAUD`.
