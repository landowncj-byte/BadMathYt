[dramatically] A gang robbed a mail train, then played Monopoly with the cash. Let's go back to England, August nineteen sixty-three. Now a Royal Mail train, the post office train, rolls through the night. [curious] It's carrying sacks of used banknotes. Guess who wants them? [conversational] A gang of about fifteen men, with a plan. [matter of fact] Step one: they rig a signal, so the driver thinks he has to stop. [playfully] Hit like if you'd never have thought of that. [conversational] It stops at a bridge in the countryside. [matter of fact] Step two: they grab a hundred and twenty mail sacks. Inside is about two point six million pounds. That's around two and a half tons of cash, heavier than a car. Step three: hide at a farmhouse. And to pass the time? They play Monopoly with the real money. [playfully] Now send this to the friend who'd do the same. [conversational] But hiding means no trace, so they wipe the place down. They planned to burn the farmhouse too. Reportedly, nobody did. [matter of fact] Then a tip sends police to the farm. They wiped almost everything— well, almost. But police found fingerprints on the Monopoly board, and on a ketchup bottle. [surprised] Wait, a board game and ketchup helped crack the case? [playfully] Follow if you love master plans beaten by board games. [seriously] In April nineteen sixty-four, a court convicted most of the gang. The main men were sentenced to up to thirty years in prison. Two and a half tons of cash, undone by a board game. [playfully] So, Monopoly or ketchup, which one was dumber?
