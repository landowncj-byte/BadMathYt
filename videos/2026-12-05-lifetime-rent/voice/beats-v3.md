## Facts / math used
ASSUMES (on screen): start $1,500/month; rent rises 3% every year (assumed); 40 years; no moves, deposits, insurance or tax angle; hypothetical, not a forecast. Math (research Python, re-checked: 18,000 × (1.03^40 − 1)/0.03 = $1,357,223): monthly rent year 10 $1,957, year 20 $2,630, year 30 $3,535, year 40 $4,751 (×3.17, "more than triple"); cumulative 10 yrs $206,350, 20 yrs $483,667, 30 yrs $856,357, 40 yrs $1,357,223 ("one point three six million"); flat rent with no raises = 1,500 × 480 = $720,000; the 3% raises add $637,223 (+88%, "almost as much as the rent itself"). Everyday: $1,500 → $4,751 a month; $720K vs $637K.

## Cautions applied
Director + research: NO rent-vs-buy verdict (never "buy a house", "renting is throwing money away", no comparison to owning); no landlord-bashing; no real city rent medians; 3% always "say"/"assume"; the salary comparison (21 years of median pay) dropped from voice to avoid overlap with the 12-05 11:00 salary Short. $1,357,223 said "one point three six million" (exact on screen); $4,751 = "forty-seven fifty-one".

## Character / photo plan
What If — no villain photo. Recurring character = YOU (same cartoon renter with a door key), a rent envelope each month, a stack of cash growing, a staircase of yearly raises; the landlord is a neutral faceless mailbox, never a villain. ASSUMES card ($1,500 start, +3%/yr assumed, 40 yrs).

## Story loop
STAKES: one envelope a month you barely think about. · QUESTION: what's the whole 40-year bill? · TWIST: $1.36 million — and the small 3% yearly raises add $637K, almost as much as the $720K of rent itself. · REHOOKS: year 10 $206K → monthly $1,957 → year 20 $484K → year 30 $856K → year 40 $4,751/month → flat vs rising.
